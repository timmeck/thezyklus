01 / THE WORLD

A sea beneath
a borrowed sky.

An ice moon. A sea beneath its frozen lid. An old people who do not know that a ship is on its way.

The Outbound has travelled for nearly two hundred years. Its people are still asleep; here, the Oruun are already tending gardens, arguing over roads and keeping supper for the late watch.

Water is a road.

Currents carry food, travellers and the work of the great wheels. Every route has its season, its resting places and someone who knows it.

Light is a life.

Honey-coloured mussels glow in woven baskets. A darkening lamp needs care, food and clean water.

A crown has weight.

Hushwheel belongs to the Crown of Nine Reaches. Mera keeps the mill; the Crown can still ask more than a small place has to give.

Find Stillwater among the moons +

Stillwater is the name humans will later put on their charts. This view shows the relationships, without claiming measured orbits.

02 / THE PEOPLE

Four arms.
Room for an answer.

Two front arms work; two rear arms hold them steady. Beneath the heavy head, two tusks curve beside a short, sensitive trunk fold.

They speak through tooth and stone, water and rail. Light moves through the patterns of their skin: feeling made visible, never proof that a word is true.

Room for you.≈

Through water

A tooth-borne impulse crosses the water. The pale wave-edged balloon is its English reading.

Just between us.

On a shared stone

Two teeth touch an answer stone. A quiet place makes room for private words.

I hear you—

Along a rail

In air, a speaking rail carries the words. Lift the contact and the sentence breaks.

English is the reading convention for their language. The tusks carry speech; their mouths do not form human words.

03 / WHO’S WHO

Neighbours first.

Eight lives around a wheel and the water roads beyond it.

Keeper of Hushwheel

Mera

She knows what a wheel needs, and how many mouths wait at home. She has plans for a second feeding channel, and keeps the first bowl she ever made.

The work must feed someone.

Guide of the water roads

Varo

Mera’s partner guides cargo through the changing currents. He leaves room for an answer, and can still lose a tool in his own home.

A place waits for you.

Examiner of the roads

Seln

Seln trusts a careful hearing more than a confident promise. She speaks plainly, and asks that a warning survive the decision made against it.

Keep my warning.

Elder and net mender

Bren

Bren has a net to mend and a supper to come home to. His measure is unreliable; his appetite and affection are not.

Save the larger bowl.

Learner at Bellwake’s reef

Tesh

Tesh tends living lamps beside her mother and longs to learn the roads. Impatient with baskets, she is patient enough with the small grazers to see a change.

Let me look for myself.

Carrier between settlements

Kell

Kell brings goods, news and the habits of distant neighbours. They give generously, but the borrowed basket still needs to come home.

The container has other plans.

Broodkeeper of the warm bank

Nell

Nell cares for the youngest in Mera and Varo’s wider household. She knows whose shell won, and makes sure the little victory keeps its proper mark.

Her shell came first.

The youngest at the table

Pebb

Pebb is Mera and Varo’s small daughter, with soft tooth buds and four short arms. A shared stone, watchful neighbours and a racing shell give her a place of her own.

A small winner.

05 / SMALL LIVES

Nothing here
is only scenery.

A lamp must eat. A reef makes a home. A tiny change can make a careful observer stop.

Lantern Mussel

A living lamp in a woven basket. Its green or honey-coloured light needs food, clean water and a lightkeeper’s care.

Threadgrazer

A small oval grazer with twelve fine foot-fringes. Whole rows follow the feeding mats and turn together at salt boundaries.

Glassroot

A living animal colony on a branching mineral frame. Its fans filter the water and shelter smaller lives.

Skitter Shell

A little flat shell that scuttles sideways over rock and tools. Children race them; elders sometimes dispute the winner.

Tassel Cleaner

A pale, comb-fine creature that eats deposits from an Oruun’s arm folds. The familiar tickle is part of ordinary care.

Basketback

A low, plated animal that helps with small loads along familiar water roads. Its harness rests on the hard plates; its soft edges need care.

Cultures at the warm banks

Warmcup

A cup-shaped animal with a pale inner lining and microbial partners. Carefully tended cultures help feed the mills.

Small lights in the water

Mirror Fry

Tiny swimmers turn together, their hard flanks catching the light. They are part of the food that keeps the living lamps alive.

A life beyond the lamps

Ribbon Herd

Long, translucent bodies graze the drifting particles of the sea. A distant passing herd makes the mill villages seem very small.
